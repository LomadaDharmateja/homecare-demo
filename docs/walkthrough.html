How a customer request turns into a ready-to-handle case

A plain-language walkthrough of the demo: what problem it solves, how it works, and four real requests followed from the moment they arrive to the moment a staff member sees them. All outputs below were produced by the running system on a laptop. Names and messages are invented.

1 · The problem

Every request is read, sorted and typed in by hand

A home-help service for elderly people gets requests by web form and email: “can someone do Mum’s shopping”, “Dad needs a lift to the doctor”, “my mother fell”. Today a staff member reads each one, decides what kind of help it is and how urgent, types it into a list, and has to remember who asked for a call back.

Urgent cases can wait in the pileA fall or missing medication sits in the same inbox as a price question, until someone happens to read it.
Callbacks get forgotten“Please call me in the afternoon” depends on someone remembering. Nobody notices when a day passes.
Time goes into copying, not caringReading, classifying and typing up each message is routine work that takes staff away from the people they help.
2 · The solution

An assistant that sorts and drafts, and people who decide

Each new message goes through the same short route. An AI model reads it and fills in a case card. Fixed rules check the card. The system sorts it by urgency and drafts a reply, and a staff member approves every reply before anything goes out. Nothing leaves the building: the AI runs on the laptop itself, not in the cloud.

Think of it like a careful new intern with a strict checklist. The intern reads every letter and fills in the form. The checklist catches the intern’s mistakes. A supervisor signs every reply. And there’s a notebook where every step is written down, so you can always see who decided what.

4 · What keeps it safe

The AI suggests. Rules check. People decide.

The AI can’t act on its ownIt can only fill in a fixed form or pick the next step from a short list. It never writes database commands and has no way to send a message.
Nothing goes out unapprovedEvery reply waits as “pending approval”. Only a staff member’s click changes that, and even “approve” sends nothing in this demo.
Everything is written downThe audit log records the incoming message, each AI choice and its stated reason, each tool’s result, every rule correction and every approval, with timestamps.
Mistakes lean towards cautionRules can raise urgency but never lower it. A case the AI can’t read at all goes to the top of the queue for a person to look at.
No data leaves the laptopThe AI model runs locally. No cloud service sees the messages, which matters for elderly people’s health information under GDPR.
5 · How well it works

Measured on requests it had never seen

Accuracy was checked with 100 invented, hand-labelled requests in English and German. The number that counts comes from 20 requests that were kept aside and never used while improving the system.

Most of the improvement didn’t come from a bigger AI. It came from letting the AI read and letting fixed rules check. For example, the AI was copying example dates from its own instructions into requests that never mentioned a date. A rule that keeps a date only if the customer wrote it fixed that class of mistake.

Where it still falls short

  • Drafted replies are the weakest part. The examples above show replies that get the callback the wrong way round or say something odd. That’s why a person approves every one.
  • German is weaker than English. The mistakes on the unseen set were German phrases (“abends” read as afternoon, “es eilt aber nicht” (no rush) still rated medium).
  • Everything is tested on invented data. Real messages are messier, so real accuracy will be lower.
  • It’s a demo: no login, no real email inbox or customer database, and approving a reply doesn’t send it.
6 · Telling it to someone else

The one-minute version

Customers write in by web form or email. Instead of staff reading and typing up each message, a small AI model running on our own computer reads it and fills in a case card: what help, how urgent, which city, whether to call back. Simple rules double-check the card against what the customer actually wrote, put urgent cases at the top and raise an alert. The system drafts a reply, but nothing is sent until a staff member approves it, and every step is logged. Each morning it lists everyone we promised to call back and haven’t. On requests it had never seen, it got every field right about 9 times out of 10 and caught every urgent case.

Demo built for an interview. Python (FastAPI, SQLite, Streamlit), a local AI model through Ollama (qwen2.5:3b), and n8n for the automation. All people and messages are fictional. Code and full technical explanation: github.com/LomadaDharmateja/homecare-demo
